Research

Three main research areas

In the CM3 Lab, we advance materials science, mechanics, and manufacturing through research in the following areas:

Physics-based modeling of materials across scales

  • From the subatomic to the macroscale, we employ a range of computational methods, including density functional theory (using VASP), atomistic simulations (using LAMMPS), coarse-grained atomistic method (using PyCAC), phase-field dislocation model (using PFDD), discrete dislocation dynamics (using OpenDiS), phase-field model (using MOOSE), crystal plasticity (using ρ-CP), and CALPHAD (using Thermo-Calc).

Physics-based modeling of additive manufacturing (AM)

  • For melt-based metal AM, we investigate both powder bed fusion and directed energy deposition, employing the discrete element method for powder dynamics (using FLOW-3D AM), thermo-fluid model for melt pool dynamics (using FLOW-3D AM), and phase-field model for solidification (using MOOSE).
  • For solid-state metal AM, we focus on cold spray, employing the discrete element method for particle dynamics (using Ansys Rocky) and computational fluid dynamics for gas flow (using Ansys Fluent).

Materials informatics

  • We establish composition-processing-structure-property relationships in materials using machine learning (ML), statistical analysis, and text mining.
  • We develop and assess ML interatomic potentials, including foundation potentials, for atomistic simulations.
  • We also develop AI-ready materials databases by using generative large language models (LLMs) to extract information from the literature.

Cross-cutting methodologies

Across our research areas, we develop and employ the following methodologies:

  • We develop agentic AI frameworks to automate and accelerate scientific research.
  • We explore quantum computing to tackle computationally challenging problems.
  • We leverage data and models to design and optimize materials.
  • We incorporate uncertainty quantification at every stage of our research.
